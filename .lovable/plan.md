## Dada Generator — Manifesto Edition

A single-page interactive and responsive website where a visitor types a few words and receives a unique, downloadable Dada manifesto page: bold constructivist typography fused with the texture of a stamped, typewritten document. The poem is generated by the original 1920 Tzara cut-up — their own words, shuffled by chance.

### The experience

1. Visitor lands on a stark, paper-textured page. A single oversized prompt sits at the top: *"Give me your words."* A textarea invites a sentence, a paragraph, anything.
2. They click **GENERATE** (or hit a giant red button stamped *MANIFESTE*).
3. The page transforms into a full-bleed broadside — masthead, columns, the cut-up poem as the lead "article," constructivist geometry slicing through it, rubber stamps and serial numbers layered on top.
4. **AGAIN** reshuffles chance and produces a new composition from the same input. **DOWNLOAD** saves it as a high-resolution PNG.
5. **NEW WORDS** clears everything and returns to the prompt.

### Visual language

A deliberate collision of two Dada-adjacent worlds:

- **Constructivist scaffolding** — bold black and oxblood-red geometry: thick diagonal bars, hard circles, arrows, and a single massive display word pulled from the user's input set in a heavy condensed sans (Druk-style, via a free alternative like Anton or Bebas Neue layered with a serif slab).
- **Stamped manifesto texture** — cream/off-white paper background with subtle grain, typewriter body text (Special Elite or Courier Prime), faux rubber-stamp circles and rectangles rotated at odd angles ("DADA," "N° 7," "REFUSÉ," dates, fake serial numbers), occasional struck-through words, ink-bleed blobs.

Every generation is composed by an algorithm, not a template — element positions, rotations, which stamps appear, where the red bar slices, which word becomes the giant headline are all chance-driven within tasteful constraints so it never looks like a layout, always like a one-off printed object.

### Poem generation — Tzara cut-up

Faithful to the 1920 recipe ("To Make a Dadaist Poem"):

1. Take the user's input.
2. Split into individual words; preserve a copy.
3. Shuffle with Fisher-Yates (seeded by `Math.random` — pure chance each time).
4. Reassemble into 8–14 lines of varying length (line breaks chosen by chance, 2–7 words each).
5. Sprinkle in chance punctuation, occasional ALL CAPS words, and 1–2 randomly struck-through words.

If the input is too short (<6 words), gently re-prompt for more. No external word banks — only the user's own language, scrambled by fate.

### Composition algorithm (visual)

A small set of deterministic-but-chance-parameterized layers, rendered to one HTML5 canvas:

1. **Paper** — cream fill + procedural grain (random noise pixels at low opacity).
2. **Headline** — pick the longest or most "interesting" word from the input, set in huge condensed type, rotated 0° or -8° or placed behind a red diagonal bar.
3. **Geometry pass** — 2–4 constructivist elements chosen from a library of svgs {thick bar, filled circle, outlined circle, arrow, triangle} in black or oxblood, placed on a loose grid.
4. **Poem columns** — typewriter font, 1 or 2 columns, slight rotation (±1°).
5. **Stamp pass** — 3–6 rubber stamps (circular and rectangular) with rotated text like "DADA," "N° 47," today's date, "REFUSÉ," "ZURICH 1916," drawn as outlined shapes with grainy fill.
6. **Ink artifacts** — 1–3 small irregular ink-blot blobs (Perlin-ish or random bezier).

All positions, rotations, sizes, and choices are randomized within hand-tuned ranges so every output feels composed, never accidental.

### Download

Render at 2× display resolution to canvas, then `canvas.toDataURL('image/png')` → trigger download as `dada-manifesto-[timestamp].png`. No external libraries needed.

### Tech notes (for the technically curious)

- Single TanStack route (`/`), one component, vanilla canvas 2D API for all drawing.
- Google Fonts loaded in `__root.tsx` head: **Anton** or **Bebas Neue** (display), **Special Elite** or **Courier Prime** (typewriter), **DM Serif Display** (accent serif).
- No npm additions required — all chance/shuffle/draw logic in plain TS modules under `src/lib/dada/` (`cutup.ts`, `compose.ts`, `draw.ts`).
- Canvas sized to a portrait broadside ratio (e.g., 1200×1600 internal, scaled responsively to viewport).
- Page metadata updated for shareability.

### Out of scope for v1

Gallery, shareable seed URLs, sound, PDF export — all easy to add later but kept out so the core loop stays sharp.
